- payment visa_validation_cache.size was 100,000 entries in the window against a median minute of 2,067 over the run, the largest change against its median of the returned series (normal: its p95 over the run is 100,000, so the state lasted long enough to be part of its own baseline) [E4].

### Hypotheses

- payment's card cache filled up ("cache full: cannot add new item"), so its Charge calls fail and checkout cannot place the order. The trace, its Log Entries and the error burst are consistent with that; they do not show why the cache filled.
- The unusual k8s.pod.memory.rss, container.memory.rss and container.memory.page_faults of payment in the same minutes are consistent with the service struggling while its cache is full; that is a correlation in time, not an established cause.

### Unknowns

- Why the cache filled, and why the burst starts and ends when it does: the logs, spans and metrics show the failure, not a configuration change or a deployment.
- Whether every error Log Entry of the burst is this same failure: grouping them by message needs full-text search, which otel_demo does not offer without reading the whole Body column.

None of the files is sorted by time or by trace_id: every row group of the log file spans nearly the whole run. Two things keep reads small instead. The Log Entry view's ts is the second-resolution TimestampTime column (13 MB for the whole file, against 305 MB for Timestamp), so counts and trends read only narrow columns. And the log and trace files carry a TraceId bloom filter in every row group, so `trace_id = <id>` reads only the row groups that may hold the trace.

## Method and limits

- The answer's numbers come from the cited Evidence in the ledger file; the selection query of step 3 is SQL, not a tool, until search_logs (T5) is merged.
- The metric rows come from a per-minute roll-up that `make otel-setup` computes once: gauges and non-monotonic sums as the mean data point, monotonic sums as the increase per minute, histograms as the mean observation (the files' sums and histograms are all cumulative). Metrics without a service (cluster and node metrics) are left out, because no span names its node.
- unusual_high and unusual_low compare the window's value with the same series' per-minute p05 and p95 over the whole 46-hour run. They describe how rare the value is, not what caused it.
- SereneDB keeps remote file data in an in-memory cache, lost when it restarts, so the bytes and server times above depend on what earlier queries left in it. A cold get_trace reads the TraceId bloom filters (about 425 MiB for both files, once; `make otel-setup` warms them) and about 20 MB for every row group that holds the trace, at about 7 MB/s from this server. The first run of this report on 2026-10-17 met a cold cache: get_trace's four remote statements for trace f6206a8a53df0b7d2e47a9cc7ebf5b11 (23 of 354 span row groups, 6 of 120 log row groups) fetched 647.60 MB from S3 in 97.37 s of server time, at most 38.21 s for one statement; the same call took 2.04 s right after. So get_trace runs each remote statement with SLM_TRACE_TIMEOUT_S (default 120 s) rather than the 15 s default.
- The Log Entry row statement reads its row groups twice (12 of 240): SereneDB first reads the columns its ORDER BY needs, then the other columns of the rows it keeps.
